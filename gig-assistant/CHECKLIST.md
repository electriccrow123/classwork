# Before you reply to any gig

The script catches common patterns. It can't catch everything. Run through this by hand.

## Walk away if any of these are true

- [ ] They want to send you a check and have you send part of it back or on to someone else.
- [ ] The "job" is receiving money or packages and forwarding them.
- [ ] They ask you to pay anything to start (fee, kit, training, software, background check).
- [ ] They ask for a bank login, SSN, card number, or a photo of your ID before any work is agreed.
- [ ] Payment involves gift cards, crypto, or a wire transfer.
- [ ] They "hire" you without ever discussing the actual work.
- [ ] The pay is far above what the work is worth.
- [ ] It's someone's graded schoolwork or exam, fake reviews, or fake accounts.

## Check before you say yes

- [ ] You can name the person or business, and they exist when you search for them.
- [ ] The deliverable, deadline and price are written down in the messages.
- [ ] You know how you'll be paid, and it's a method with a paper trail (PayPal/Venmo/Zelle
      invoice, or a freelance platform's escrow).
- [ ] For a new client: part payment up front, or a small first milestone.
- [ ] If the post says "no AI", you either don't take it or you ask first.

## Before you deliver

- [ ] You read every word and ran every piece of code yourself.
- [ ] Facts, names, numbers and links are checked. AI output can be confidently wrong.
- [ ] Nothing is copied from someone else's work.
- [ ] You keep a copy of what you sent and when.

## Money

- [ ] Only ever give out what's needed to *receive* a payment (a PayPal email, a Zelle
      phone number). Never a login, never a card number.
- [ ] Wait for a payment to actually clear before treating it as yours. Checks can bounce
      weeks later.
- [ ] Keep a simple log of income. Freelance income is taxable.
- [ ] If you're under 18, most payment apps and freelance platforms need a parent or
      guardian on the account.
